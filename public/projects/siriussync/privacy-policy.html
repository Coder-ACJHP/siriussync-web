1. Data Protection Roles: Data Controller vs. Software Provider (GDPR & KVKK)

Under the Turkish Law on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK Law No. 6698) and the European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR Regulation (EU) 2016/679), legal roles and responsibilities are explicitly delineated:

  • Data Controller (Customer Enterprise): The commercial enterprise or business entity licensing and deploying SiriusSync ITS acts as the Data Controller. The customer enterprise defines the business purposes, scope of data collection, operator role authorizations, data retention schedules, and lawful grounds for all commercial records and personal data managed within its deployment.
  • Software Provider (Developer): Onur Işık (Coder ACJHP) acts solely as the Software Provider furnishing the desktop application software. In standard commercial operations, the Developer maintains no access to customer databases, operates no centralized surveillance or telemetric logging infrastructure, and does not act as a Data Controller for enterprise business records.

2. Categories of Personal & Operational Data Processed

SiriusSync ITS processes the following categories of operational and commercial data:

  • User Accounts & Authentication Credentials: Enterprise operator profiles, administrative accounts, role assignments, usernames, and passwords. Passwords are never stored or transmitted in plain text; they are secured using one-way salted BCrypt hashing with a work factor of 12 (12 rounds) on the client workstation prior to verification against backend records.
  • Commercial ERP & Transactional Records: Core operational records including stock inventory levels, product catalogs (SKUs, barcodes, dimensions, weights, unit costs, pricing), Customer Relationship Management (CRM) contacts (client and supplier names, corporate titles, credit limits, outstanding balances, interaction history), Sales Order Management (SOM) quotations, orders, and delivery notes, Purchase Order Management (POM) procurement workflows, goods receipt notes (GRN), multi-item financial receipts, and cost sheets.
  • Cryptographic Hardware Fingerprinting (HWID Licensing): To enforce workstation device seat limits (e.g., up to three (3) workstation seats depending on your licensed tier), the Application generates a cryptographic Hardware Identifier (HWID) using the open-source OSHI (Operating System and Hardware Information) library. The HWID is an irreversible mathematical hash derived from physical hardware components (motherboard and processor serials) and collects no personal, behavioral, or browsing data.
  • Media Uploads & Document Attachments: Product imagery, scanned supplier invoices, and receipt attachments uploaded by authorized operators are stored in dedicated cloud object storage buckets (Supabase Storage) linked directly to the customer’s cloud project.
  • Voluntary Communications & Support Data: Information you voluntarily provide when contacting the Developer for technical support, licensing inquiries, or bug reports (such as your corporate email address, communication history, and voluntary diagnostic error snippets).
  • Strict Zero-Telemetry Guarantee: The desktop software contains NO behavioral tracking scripts, NO analytics libraries, NO third-party advertising SDKs, and places NO tracking cookies on client machines.

3. Lawful Bases for Processing (GDPR Article 6 & KVKK Article 5)

Where the Software Provider processes personal data (such as licensing verification and technical support communications), processing is strictly justified under the following lawful bases:

  • Performance of a Contract (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b) / KVKK Art. 5(2)(c)): Processing is necessary to deliver, license, authenticate, and validate workstation seats for SiriusSync ITS pursuant to the Terms of Use.
  • Legitimate Interests (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f) / KVKK Art. 5(2)(f)): Processing cryptographic HWID hashes and security logs is necessary for our legitimate commercial interests in software license enforcement, fraud prevention, intellectual property protection, and system security.
  • Compliance with Legal Obligations (GDPR Art. 6(1)(c) / KVKK Art. 5(2)(a)): Retaining licensing transaction records to comply with applicable tax, commercial accounting, and regulatory obligations.
  • Consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a) / KVKK Art. 5(1)): Where explicit consent is provided by a data subject for specific voluntary support or feedback interactions.

4. Technical, Physical & Organizational Security Controls

SiriusSync ITS enforces robust multi-layered architectural safeguards to protect enterprise data confidentiality, availability, and integrity:

  • Zero Telemetry by Design: Absolutely zero background phone-home mechanisms, no demographic data collection, and no background telemetry.
  • Closed Database Direct Ports: Direct public database ports (including standard PostgreSQL port 5432) remain strictly closed to the public internet. The desktop client never connects directly to raw database ports, shielding your core database against brute-force attacks and network sniffing.
  • HTTPS / TLS 1.3 & Secure WebSockets Only: All client-to-backend communications flow exclusively through encrypted HTTPS endpoints utilizing TLS 1.3 (via PostgREST APIs) and authenticated, encrypted WebSocket (WSS) channels for real-time multi-client synchronization.
  • Client-Side BCrypt Hashing: Credentials are hashed locally on the user’s machine with salted BCrypt (12 rounds) before transmission, ensuring plaintext passwords never touch the network or database.
  • Immutable Audit Trails (activity_logs): Critical business events (such as inventory adjustments, order stage transitions, permission modifications, and deletions) are committed to the activity_logs table as immutable, append-only records to guarantee forensic auditability and fraud prevention.
  • Row-Level Security (RLS): Granular PostgreSQL Row-Level Security policies ensure data isolation and protect against unauthorized cross-tenant or unauthenticated data manipulation.

5. Automated Orphaned Media Cleanup Policy

To preserve cloud storage integrity, reduce unnecessary data retention, and maintain hygiene, SiriusSync ITS incorporates an automated media cleanup routine. When an inventory item, financial receipt, or ERP record with associated cloud attachments is deleted by an authorized operator, the corresponding unreferenced image and document files in Supabase Object Storage are automatically and permanently purged.

6. Third-Party Cloud Infrastructure (Supabase)

SiriusSync ITS interfaces with Supabase for managed PostgreSQL database services, PostgREST API handling, Realtime WebSocket synchronization, and object storage.

The customer enterprise establishes and maintains its own dedicated Supabase project instance. All enterprise records and media files stored in Supabase remain subject to the customer’s cloud configuration, access keys, and Supabase’s data security and compliance standards. The Developer does not possess backdoor access to customer project instances in standard operational use.

9. Data Subject Rights (GDPR Articles 15–22 & KVKK Article 11)

Under Turkish KVKK (Law No. 6698, Article 11) and European GDPR (Articles 15–22), data subjects whose personal data is processed possess the following legal rights:

  • Right of Access & Information: Request confirmation as to whether personal data is processed, and obtain clear details concerning the purposes, categories, and recipients of such data;
  • Right to Rectification: Request the correction of incomplete, out-of-date, or inaccurate personal data;
  • Right to Erasure (“Right to be Forgotten”): Request the permanent deletion, destruction, or anonymization of personal data when legal retention grounds expire;
  • Right to Restriction of Processing: Request the suspension of processing while the accuracy of data or legitimate interests are contested;
  • Right to Data Portability: Request copies of structured personal data in a readable format, facilitated via built-in system tools (including Apache POI Excel export and JasperReports PDF generation);
  • Right to Object: Object at any time to data processing carried out under legitimate interest grounds;
  • Right to Withdraw Consent: Withdraw consent at any time where processing was founded upon consent;
  • Right to Lodge a Complaint: Lodge a complaint with the competent supervisory authority:
    • In Turkey: Personal Data Protection Authority (Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu — kvkk.gov.tr);
    • In the EU/EEA: The competent national Data Protection Authority in your habitual place of residence;
    • In the United Kingdom: The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO — ico.org.uk).

Because the customer enterprise acts as the Data Controller for its operational deployment, individual operators and counterparties should submit data subject requests directly to their organization’s designated system administrator. For software-level questions, contact our support team.
